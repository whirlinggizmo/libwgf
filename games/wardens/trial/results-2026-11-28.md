# Wardens: libwgf beside PixiJS 3D and three.js (2026-11-28)

What an agent pays to build Wardens with libwgf, beside two engines for the browser, under the same conditions. Nine fresh Sonnet agents
built helix's Wardens (its `docs/trials/templates/wardens/spec.md`, with its assets), three on each engine, 3 hours each at most, with
`tools/run_entrant.py` (a bubblewrap sandbox, a private display drawn on the CPU, no sound, an empty home):

- **libwgf**: natively in Haxe on libwgf's binding, each on a worktree of libwgf at `5d5af09` of its own, with [brief.md](brief.md):
  the binding's API files, libwgf's C examples, and `docs/llms.txt`, an index of the two for agents (new since the last round).
- **PixiJS 3D**: PixiJS 8.23 and PixiJS 3D 0.1 in a page, with [pixi/brief.md](pixi/brief.md): PixiJS's docs for agents, mirrored
  from its `llms.txt` (1,074 pages, PixiJS 3D's among them), as PixiJS 3D is newer than the model's training.
- **three.js**: three.js r187 in a page, with helix's three.js brief unchanged: three.js's sources alone.

A page entrant bundles and runs its game in a headless Chromium (`node run.cjs`, helix's). No entrant fetched anything from the web.
Graded as helix grades: screenshots (helix's `take_screenshots.py` on the native programs; `tools/create_grading_shots.py`, the same
shots and input in a browser, on the pages), then a fresh agent each with helix's `review-brief.md`, its source line naming `src/`'s
`.js` files for a page, reading no notes. The runs went in three waves of one of each, from 04:27 to 05:24 EST, on coinspinner.

## What it found

**three.js costs less than half of either.** $1.16 a run against PixiJS 3D's $2.51 and libwgf's $2.86, in half the time, reading a
seventh as much (1.7M cache read against 8.2M and 11.2M). The model knows three.js from training and wrote it without looking
anything up; PixiJS 3D and libwgf it read. Its output was the least too (59k against 81k and 83k): shorter programs, 1,100 lines
against libwgf's 2,000.

**Their grades are close.** 34.0, 35.7 and 34.7 of 37; each engine's runs spread by more than the gaps between engines (three.js's
32 to 36, libwgf's 33 to 36). This round's libwgf runs graded lower than the last round's 36 each, with the same brief but for
`llms.txt`, so the spread is the runs', not the index's.

**Item 4 in a browser is the spec's, not the entrants'.** The spec has Play go fullscreen and Escape pause. In a browser the first
Escape in fullscreen is the browser's: it leaves fullscreen and the page never sees the key. So five of six page runs were graded
wrong on item 4 (the pause panel missing a second after Escape), each right in its code; pixi.001, which went straight into play
from its query and never fullscreen, was graded met. A page can take Escape in fullscreen with the Keyboard Lock API
(`navigator.keyboard.lock(['Escape'])`), but no entrant could see the problem, as `run.cjs` sends no input. The column without
item 4 counts the other 36.

**What the agents met** (their notes, read after grading):

- **libwgf had what the others lacked, for the characters.** Every PixiJS 3D and three.js run wrote its own way to put a character's
  four part files on one skeleton and to attack from the waist up while the legs run: neither engine has a bone mask, and PixiJS 3D's
  clips name nodes by a file's own indices, so two of its runs wrote their own animation sampler (`src/anim.js`, `src/char.js`). The
  libwgf runs used `playLayerAnimation` on the `Abdomen` bone and the parts as models, and wrote nothing of the kind.
- **No health bar in the world, again, though libwgf has one.** Two libwgf runs drew it by hand (two tilted `Shape3d` rectangles; a
  2D rectangle at `worldToScreen`). A sprite on a 3D stage faces the camera, but `llms.txt`'s line for `Sprite` says only "on a 2D or
  3D stage", and nothing says billboard. pixi.003 used PixiJS 3D's billboard quads.
- **No atlas region for the immediate UI.** `Ui.tile` and `Ui.iconButton` take a whole texture, so libwgf.009 couldn't show
  `icons.png`'s cells on a card and drew every menu itself with `Draw.textureNineSlice` and `Draw.textureRegion`.
- **A flash for one enemy of many: three ways.** A custom material swapped in for 0.1 s (007), a material for each enemy (008), and the
  tint's green channel read by the shader as the flash (009). PixiJS 3D and three.js runs swapped a second shared material, or set a
  uniform before each draw (three.001's, graded wrong as it flashed every enemy).
- **A relative path saves under `assets/`.** Two runs' screenshots, saved by a plain path, landed in the asset folder, as the file
  system's root is the asset host's.
- **Pausing: two calls.** Two runs paused with `Actor.setPaused` on the stage, one with `Loop.setTimeScale(0)`; 009 noted that the time
  scale stops ticks but not animation.
- **A warning at exit.** libwgf.007's program logs `wgf_resource_release: ... isn't a resource` once as it ends, from one of its
  shader releases: not looked into yet.
- **PixiJS 3D's own surprises:** `Scene3DSource.animations` is a record by name, not an array (two runs); `worldToScreen` is null
  before the first render; `Skeleton.indexOf` knows joints only; PixiJS has no sound (Web Audio by hand, each run). three.js r187's
  `THREE.Clock` is deprecated (two runs).

**The failed builds** were one each for the libwgf runs, all Haxe's (an array of mixed types, `Float should be Int`, an
abstract's field called statically), and one for pixi.001, an import PixiJS 8 doesn't export (`Canvas`); none for three.js.
